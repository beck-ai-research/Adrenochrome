## 1. PolitiFact Analysis: Diddy Adrenochrome Book Hoax (2024)

### Case Summary
False claim that Sean "Diddy" Combs wrote a book titled "The Adrenochrome Witch" circulated on Instagram in September 2024. This claim was part of broader QAnon conspiracy theories about celebrity involvement in adrenochrome harvesting.

### Investigation Findings
- **Barnes & Noble Statement:** "This is not an authentic book and we do not have it listed on our website"
- **Logo Analysis:** Image featured incorrect Barnes & Noble logo
- **Platform Investigation:** Archived sample page on Nook for Web was fake, removed in July 2020
- **Self-Publishing Exploitation:** Barnes & Noble Press platform allows anyone to upload books, enabling hoaxes

### Legal Assessment
- **Rating:** FALSE
- **Evidence:** No legitimate publishing record, no media coverage, official retailer denial
- **Pattern:** Part of ongoing QAnon celebrity targeting with adrenochrome accusations

## 2. AP News Analysis: "Save the Children" Co-opting (2020)

### Case Summary
QAnon movement successfully co-opted legitimate child protection slogan "Save the Children" for conspiracy theory promotion, causing confusion with legitimate organizations.

### Investigation Findings
- **Polaris Hotline Impact:** National Human Trafficking Hotline received hundreds of calls about QAnon misinformation
- **Myth-Busting Response:** Nonprofit created dedicated resource page to counter QAnon disinformation
- **Slogan Hijacking:** Legitimate child protection phrase weaponized for conspiracy theories
- **Physical Manifestations:** Street protests with "Save Our Children" signs promoting QAnon narratives

### Legal Assessment
- **Organization Response:** Legitimate child protection groups forced to create myth-busting resources
- **Public Confusion:** Deliberate blurring of lines between real activism and conspiracy promotion
- **Platform Amplification:** Social media algorithms contributed to slogan's viral spread
